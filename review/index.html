Finding oneTen seconds is a long time to keep a booking.

On a mobile connection the flagship home takes past ten seconds to render its main image, holds the main thread blocking for more than two seconds, and shifts under the reader's thumb as it settles. A guest who arrived from a paid search ad or an Instagram story is, by then, already gone.

Google's own research puts numbers on it: as mobile load moves from one second to three, the probability of a bounce rises by 32%; from one to five seconds, by 90% (Google / SOASTA, 2017). Separately, Google reports that 53% of mobile visits are abandoned when a page takes longer than three seconds (Google / DoubleClick, 2016). The traffic is already paid for; the speed is where it is lost.

Finding twoThe home market cannot read the site.

Across banyantree.com, angsana.com and cassia.com the language set is identical: English, Chinese and Korean. There is no Thai. The Bangkok, Phuket, Samui and Krabi property pages, the spa pages and the dining pages all serve in English to a Thai visitor. The residences site does ship Thai, which shows the capability exists within the group; the hotel brands' guest and booking surface does not yet carry it.

Thai travellers are a significant and high-intent domestic market for staycations, spa and dining, and they search, compare and decide in Thai. A page that does not speak the language is rarely the one that gets the booking, and it is invisible to Thai-language search from the start.

Finding threeThe path into booking leaves the brand.

From a property page, the booking action hands the guest across to a separate domain, a generic reservation engine, in English only, that greets them under a different company's loyalty programme. Dining reservations hand off again to a third domain. Each jump is a moment where the confidence the brand just built has to survive a plain, unfamiliar screen.

The dates and property carry across in the link, which is good work. What does not carry is the brand, the language and the sense of one continuous room. Every context switch is a place a nearly-made booking can quietly end.
